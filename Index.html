Nigeria has a rich history shaped by ancient kingdoms, diverse cultures, colonial rule, independence and the contributions of millions of Nigerians.

Explore important events that helped shape our nation.

Explore Nigeria's History

Before the 1800s — Ancient Civilisations

The Nok culture is known for its remarkable terracotta sculptures. Powerful societies and kingdoms, including Ife and Benin, developed distinctive art, trade and political systems.

1800s — Kingdoms and Changing Trade

The Sokoto Caliphate emerged in the early nineteenth century. Oyo, Benin and other states also played important roles in the region's political and cultural history.

1861–1914 — British Colonial Expansion

Britain annexed Lagos in 1861. Colonial control expanded across the region, and in 1914 the Northern and Southern Protectorates were amalgamated into Nigeria.

1 October 1960 — Independence

Nigeria gained independence from Britain on 1 October 1960. Sir Abubakar Tafawa Balewa became the country's first prime minister.

1967–1970 — Nigerian Civil War

The Nigerian Civil War followed the attempted secession of Biafra. The war ended in January 1970 and left a lasting impact on the country.

1 October 1963 — Republic

Nigeria became a republic in 1963, replacing the British monarch as head of state with a Nigerian president.

29 May 1999 — Return to Civilian Rule

Nigeria returned to democratic civilian government under President Olusegun Obasanjo after years of military rule.
