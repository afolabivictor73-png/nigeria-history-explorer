Discover Nigeria's History

Search for a topic or explore the historical periods below. Nigerian history includes many peoples, languages, cultures and political traditions.

Ancient Civilizations

1. Early Nigerian Societies

Human communities have lived in the region now called Nigeria for thousands of years. Archaeological discoveries, including the famous Dufuna canoe, provide evidence of early life and technological skills.

The Nok culture, known especially for its terracotta sculptures and early ironworking evidence, flourished in parts of central Nigeria during the first millennium BCE and the first millennium CE.

👑
Kingdoms and Empires

2. Great Nigerian Kingdoms

Before colonial rule, the region contained influential political and cultural centers. The Kingdom of Benin became famous for its court art and bronze and brass works.

Ile-Ife became a major center of Yoruba culture and remarkable artistic traditions. The Oyo Empire developed an influential political system, while Kanem-Bornu and other northern states participated in regional trade and scholarship.

📚
Trade and Scholarship

3. Trade, Religion and Culture

Communities across the region developed trade networks linking markets, farming areas and long-distance commercial routes. Trans-Saharan trade connected northern communities with North Africa and wider commercial networks.

Islam spread through parts of northern Nigeria over many centuries. Christianity later expanded in many southern communities, particularly through nineteenth-century missions and subsequent developments.

🗺️
Colonial Period

4. British Colonial Rule

British political and commercial influence expanded during the nineteenth century. Following military campaigns and colonial agreements, Britain established control over territories that became Nigeria.

The Northern and Southern Protectorates were amalgamated in 1914 under Frederick Lugard. Colonial administration changed political institutions and the economy, while Nigerians continued to resist, negotiate and demand greater rights.

🇳🇬
Independence

5. The Road to Freedom

Nigerian nationalists, workers, journalists, students, traditional leaders and political organizations contributed to the struggle for self-government.

Nigeria became independent on 1 October 1960. Nnamdi Azikiwe became Governor-General, while Abubakar Tafawa Balewa served as Prime Minister. Nigeria became a republic in 1963.

⚔️
Conflict and Change

6. Civil War and Reconstruction

Political crises, military coups and regional tensions preceded the Nigerian Civil War of 1967–1970, fought between the Federal Government and the secessionist state of Biafra.

The war caused enormous suffering and loss of life. It ended in January 1970 with the surrender of Biafran forces. Postwar reconstruction and national reconciliation became major challenges.

🗳️
Democracy

7. Military Rule and Democratic Return

Nigeria experienced several periods of military government after independence, alongside brief civilian administrations. These years shaped debates about constitutional government, civil liberties and national unity.

Civilian democratic government returned on 29 May 1999, beginning the current continuous period of the Fourth Republic.

🌍
Contemporary Nigeria

8. Nigeria in the Modern World

Contemporary Nigeria is a federal republic with 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja. Its people contribute to agriculture, business, science, education, technology, sports, literature and the arts.

The country continues to address challenges involving security, economic development, public services, governance and social inclusion, while its citizens work toward different visions for the nation's future.

Nigeria: A Historical Timeline

Explore some important dates and turning points in Nigerian history. Earlier dates are approximate where indicated.

c. 1500 BCE–500 CE — Nok Culture

Archaeological evidence of Nok culture includes distinctive terracotta sculptures and evidence of ironworking in parts of central Nigeria. Dating and interpretations vary by site.

Medieval Centuries — Regional Kingdoms

Benin, Ife, Oyo, Kanem-Bornu and numerous other societies developed distinctive political institutions, artistic traditions and trade networks over different periods.

1804 — Sokoto Jihad Begins

Usman dan Fodio led a religious and political movement that resulted in the establishment of the Sokoto Caliphate and major political changes across parts of northern Nigeria.

1851 — British Attack on Lagos

British forces attacked Lagos during a period of expanding British influence. Lagos was subsequently annexed by Britain in 1861.

1914 — Amalgamation

Britain amalgamated the Northern and Southern Protectorates, creating the colonial administrative unit known as Nigeria.

1 October 1960 — Independence

Nigeria became an independent country after British colonial rule.

1 October 1963 — Republic

Nigeria became a republic, replacing the British monarch as its formal head of state.

1967–1970 — Nigerian Civil War

A devastating civil war ended in January 1970 following the surrender of Biafran forces.

29 May 1999 — Fourth Republic

Civilian government returned, beginning Nigeria's current continuous democratic era.
