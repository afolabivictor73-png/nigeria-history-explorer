Important milestones

Nigeria's Historical Timeline

Follow key events that influenced the development of Nigeria.

c. 1500 BCE onward

Nok Culture

The Nok culture is famous for terracotta sculptures and evidence of early ironworking. Its dates and development remain subjects of research.

c. 1100–1400 CE

Ile-Ife's Artistic Traditions

Ile-Ife became a major Yoruba cultural and political centre, renowned for extraordinary naturalistic sculptures.

Before colonial rule

Powerful African States

The Benin Kingdom, Oyo Empire, Kanem-Bornu and other states developed distinctive political systems, trade networks and cultures.

1804 onward

Fulani Jihad and Sokoto Caliphate

The movement led by Usman dan Fodio transformed political and religious life across much of northern Nigeria.

1914

Amalgamation of Nigeria

British colonial authorities joined the Northern and Southern Protectorates into one administrative colony under Frederick Lugard.

1 October 1960

Independence

Nigeria gained independence from Britain, beginning a new chapter as a sovereign country.

1 October 1963

Nigeria Becomes a Republic

Nigeria became a republic, replacing the British monarch as its ceremonial head of state.

1967–1970

Nigerian Civil War

The conflict, also known as the Biafran War, caused immense suffering and remains an important part of national history.

29 May 1999

Return to Civilian Rule

Nigeria returned to civilian democratic government after years of military rule.

Today

Modern Nigeria

Nigeria continues to develop through its diverse communities, institutions, arts, education, technology and economic activity.

History library

More Chapters of Our History

Read short introductions to important themes in Nigeria's past.

1. Life Before Colonial Rule

Before British colonial rule, the region now called Nigeria contained many societies with their own political systems, languages, economies and beliefs. People farmed, traded, made crafts, fished and built networks linking communities across West Africa.

2. The Transatlantic Slave Trade

For centuries, the transatlantic slave trade forcibly transported millions of Africans across the Atlantic. Coastal areas of present-day Nigeria were connected to this tragic history. The trade affected families, communities, politics and regional economies, and Africans also resisted enslavement in many ways.

3. Resistance to Colonial Rule

Colonial rule faced resistance from different communities and leaders. One notable event was the Women's War of 1929 in southeastern Nigeria, when women protested colonial policies and the threat of taxation. Their actions became an important part of Nigeria's history of resistance.

4. The Road to Independence

Nigerian nationalists, labour groups, women, students, journalists and political organisations campaigned for greater self-government. Constitutional changes gradually expanded Nigerian participation in government before independence on 1 October 1960.

5. The Nigerian Civil War

The Nigerian Civil War lasted from 1967 to 1970 after the southeastern region declared itself the Republic of Biafra. The war caused widespread suffering, displacement and hunger. Its legacy remains important to conversations about national unity, justice and reconciliation.

6. Nigeria's Fourth Republic

On 29 May 1999, Nigeria returned to civilian democratic government, beginning the Fourth Republic. Since then, elections, constitutional institutions, public debate and civic participation have shaped the country's political development.

7. Nigeria Today

Modern Nigeria is home to many ethnic groups and languages, with influential traditions in music, literature, film, fashion, technology, sport and entrepreneurship. Understanding the past can help people explore the challenges and possibilities facing the country today.
