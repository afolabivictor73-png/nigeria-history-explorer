The History of Idanre

Exploring the people, traditions, royal institution and heritage of Idanre.

Community History

Origins and Historical Traditions

Idanre is a historic Yoruba community in present-day Ondo State, southwestern Nigeria. Its history is preserved through oral traditions, cultural practices, traditional institutions and historical scholarship.

Accounts of Idanre's origins include traditions connecting its people with the wider Yoruba historical world. Such accounts should be studied alongside local oral histories and documentary evidence because different traditions may explain the community's beginnings in different ways.

Traditional Institution

The Owa of Idanre

The Owa is the traditional ruler of Idanre. The royal institution forms an important part of the community's political and cultural heritage.

Research into the Owa institution should consider royal traditions, the responsibilities of traditional authorities, relationships with community chiefs, and the ways leadership and local administration have changed over time.

Royal History

The Succession of Owa Kings

The succession of Idanre's Owa kings deserves a dedicated historical record. Each ruler's name, alternative spelling, accession, reign dates and relationship to the preceding ruler should be checked against reliable local and documentary sources.

A complete chronological list has not been included here because the available evidence must first be checked. This avoids presenting an unverified royal genealogy as established fact.

Research priority: Consult the palace's historical records, documented oral traditions, relevant archival reports and scholarly publications.

Landscape and Heritage

The Idanre Hills and Historic Settlement

The Idanre Hills are a distinctive landscape associated with Idanre's cultural identity and settlement history. The historic settlement and its landscape offer important opportunities to study how communities interacted with their physical environment.

Historical accounts of settlement, movement and community life should be considered alongside archaeological evidence, local knowledge and documented traditions.

Social and Economic History

Life in Precolonial Idanre

Historical research describes precolonial Idanre as a society in which household farming, cooperation among family members, age groups and communal production were important to economic and social life.

The colonial period brought significant changes to the local economy, including the expansion of cocoa production and commercial agriculture.
